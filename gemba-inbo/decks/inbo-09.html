Models of innovation

Idea generation is a stochastic search for the best alternative.

  1. Random payoffs. Weitzman (1979) — optimal search; links to bandit problems, exploration vs. exploitation.
  2. Applied in new-product development. Dahan & Mendelson (2001), Terwiesch & Loch (2004), Chick, Forster & Pertile (2017).
  3. Tested experimentally. Girotra et al. (2010) — in the context of innovation, it is the extreme that matters, not the average.

Hypotheses · how AI impacts creativity

Three hypotheses, before we look at results.

H0

Fluency

  • The capacity for idea generation increases with the use of ChatGPT.
  • Number of ideas generated, and the cost per idea.
H1

Quality

  • The average quality of AI-generated ideas is at least as good as the average quality of human-generated ideas.
H2

Diversity

  • The diversity of AI-generated ideas is lower than human ideas — and their average novelty is lower too.

Strengths and limits of the virtual survey

What we say and what we do are not the same gap an LLM can close.

  1. Even GPT-3.5, in practice: Predicts customer behavior and price sensitivity by income — but skews Western and affluent, and quietly fills in missing data.
  2. Observation. A student replicated the Milgram experiment with a similar outcome — one that would never pass an IRB with real subjects.
  3. The open question. Survey answers don’t always match observed behavior. Does that gap carry over to LLMs?

Implications for innovation management

The four levers, revisited — with AI as a teammate.

The four levers, with AI

Fluency: LLM as a support tool. Mean: LLM lifts average quality with context. Variance: chain-of-reasoning helps, but humans stay more diverse and novel — for now. Accuracy: both humans and LLMs mis-forecast purchase intent.

Top ideas: LLM is efficient at finding valuable areas; human insight still matters.

Caveats

LLMs are evolving quickly. Cosine similarity may not capture the full diversity of external context. Screening iterates — generate, evaluate, develop, evaluate again. Keep a human in the loop.

More to do: learning how this integrates across multiple stages of screening and pivoting.
